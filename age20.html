Age 20

I wanted to be a software engineer.

I imagined building things important enough for millions of people to use.

I imagined coding as a puzzle, where every problem had a logical solution.

I did not imagine how lonely it could feel to spend hours debugging something invisible.

I spent nights learning languages, frameworks, and technologies to push myself ahead of the curve.

I slowly realized that tech culture often confuses productivity with busyness.

At some point, I raelized I cared more about the people using the product than the code itself.

I wanted to understand humans as much as I wanted to understand systems.
